# Week 5 — Percentage, Ratio & Proportion, Profit/Loss & Simple Interest — Answer Key

**Q1. C) 70** — 20% of 350 = 0.20 × 350 = 70.

**Q2. B) 40%** — 2/5 = 0.4 = 40%.

**Q3. B) 25%** — 45/180 × 100 = 25%.

**Q4. C) 36** — 5 parts = 45 boys, so 1 part = 9; girls = 4 parts = 36.

**Q5. C) ₹360** — Total parts = 5, so 1 part = ₹120; B's share = 3 parts = ₹360.

**Q6. C) ₹105** — Cost per pen = ₹45 ÷ 3 = ₹15; 7 pens = 7 × ₹15 = ₹105.

**Q7. B) 20%** — Profit = ₹300 − ₹250 = ₹50; profit % = 50/250 × 100 = 20%.

**Q8. B) 20%** — Loss % = 40/200 × 100 = 20%.

**Q9. C) ₹1,200** — SI = (Principal × Rate × Time) ÷ 100 = (5000 × 8 × 3) ÷ 100 = ₹1,200.

**Q10. B) 5%** — Rate = (SI × 100) ÷ (Principal × Time) = (500 × 100) ÷ (2000 × 5) = 5%.

**Q11. C) 288** — 60% of 480 = 0.6 × 480 = 288.

**Q12. C) 10%** — Loss = ₹450 − ₹405 = ₹45; loss % = 45/450 × 100 = 10%.

**Q13. C) 77** — Total parts = 4+7 = 11, so 1 part = 121÷11 = 11; larger number = 7 × 11 = 77.

**Q14. C) ₹9,600** — SI = (8000 × 10 × 2) ÷ 100 = ₹1,600; amount = 8000 + 1600 = ₹9,600.

**Q15. B) ₹400** — Marked price = 400 × 1.25 = ₹500; after a 20% discount: 500 × 0.8 = ₹400. The selling price ends up exactly equal to the cost price — a 25% markup followed by a 20% discount cancel out exactly (1.25 × 0.8 = 1), so there's neither profit nor loss. This is a classic "trick" to watch for: a bigger-sounding percentage off doesn't always mean a lower final price than you started with.
